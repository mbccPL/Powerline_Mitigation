Project

Power line mitigation

MBCC is a leading wildlife conservation organization focused on protecting birds and their habitats in Mongolia. We work in partnership with government agencies, energy companies, and international funds to reduce human-caused bird mortality.

🦅 Our Mission

To conserve Mongolia's rich bird diversity through research, mitigation, and community engagement — with a particular focus on raptors threatened by modern infrastructure.

🤝 Key Partners

We collaborate with the Ministry of Environment and Climate Change, Mohamed Bin Zayed Raptor Conservation Fund (MBZRCF), regional electricity distribution companies, and other partners.

📍 Project Area

Our powerline mitigation work spans Mongolia's steppe zone — the highest-risk region where abundant rodent prey attracts large numbers of raptors to distribution lines.

🔬 Science-Based Approach

All mitigation methods are field-tested, peer-reviewed, and comply with MNS 6518:2015 — Mongolia's national standard for bird-safe power infrastructure.

The Project

Govisumber Railway Powerline Mitigation

In partnership with Choir Railway, MBCC is retrofitting concrete railway power poles in Govisumber Province with insulation covers — eliminating electrocution risk for birds perching on these structures.

⚡

The Problem

Concrete poles with steel crossarms and upright pin-insulators create a lethal gap between energized conductors. Birds perching on the pole top bridge this gap and are electrocuted.

🛡️

The Solution

MBZ2 insulation covers — pole cap covers and crossarm top/bottom covers — physically insulate all non-conducting elements, eliminating contact risk at all three phases.

🚂

Our Partner

Choir Railway supplies power to communities along the Govisumber–Shivee Ovoo corridor. UB Railway engineers install the covers under MBCC supervision.

📦

Supply Chain

Covers are manufactured in China to MBZRCF specifications, using ASA plastic rated for 70+ year outdoor lifespan and temperatures from −50°C to +70°C.

📏

Scale

The project covers two power lines: Line 1 (885 poles, fully mitigated) and Line 2 (285 poles, installation ongoing). A total of 1,170 poles across both lines.

✅

Progress

Line 1 is 100% mitigated. Line 2 is 73% complete (209 of 285 poles). Remaining 76 poles are scheduled for the next installation phase.

Mitigation

How Bird-Safe Covers Work

Electrocution occurs when a bird simultaneously contacts two energized conductors, or one conductor and a grounded surface. Insulation covers eliminate this risk by covering all dangerous non-conducting elements on the pole.

1

Pole cap cover (MBZ2)

A two-piece insulated cap is fitted over the pole top, covering all exposed galvanized steel mounts and the concrete pole crown. This prevents birds from contacting the top-phase conductor while perching.

2

Crossarm top cover

A two-piece sheath clips over the top of the galvanized steel crossarm. Birds often perch on the crossarm directly below the pin-insulators — this cover eliminates that contact point.

3

Crossarm bottom cover

The underside of the crossarm is also covered. Phase II and III conductor wires run below the crossarm; larger birds (eagles, buzzards) can span this gap. The bottom cover closes this electrocution pathway.

4

Anchor pole treatment

Anchor poles receive a pole cap cover and jumper wire (ARK sheath) insulation on all conductor connections. Crossarm covers are not required on anchor poles by design.

5

Full MBZ2 mitigation = bird-safe

A pole is considered fully mitigated only when pole cap cover AND both crossarm covers are installed together. Partial installations — or brush deterrents alone — do not meet the MBZ2 standard.

📋 Mongolian Bird-Safe Powerline Standard — MNS 6518:2015, Appendix D

  • Insulation materials must withstand temperatures from −50°C to +70°C and winds up to 40 m/s
  • Design lifespan: 25–35 years; covers must be rated for 6–15 kV lines
  • Pole top cover required on all line poles to prevent birds perching on exposed steel mounts
  • Phase II and III conductors must be positioned at least 50 cm below the crossarm to prevent double-contact electrocution (D.1.2)
  • Crossarm covers required when crossarm-to-conductor spacing is less than 1.5 metres (D.1.3)
  • All conductor connections at anchor poles must be insulated with covered cable (D.2.2)
  • Connections at substations and additional equipment must also be insulated (D.2.3)

Research

Science Behind the Work

Our field surveys and data collection support both project implementation and broader scientific understanding of raptor electrocution in Mongolia.

Background

Scale of the Problem

Abu Dhabi-funded research estimated that over 4,000 Saker Falcons were among approximately 18,000 raptors electrocuted annually in Mongolia before mitigation efforts began. Electrocution rates correlate with rodent prey abundance along powerlines.

Methods

Audit Survey Methodology

Five field teams inspected 69 power lines across 15 provinces in August 2023. Data collected: pole type, mitigation presence and type, cover condition, and carcass counts under each pole surveyed.

Materials

ASA Insulation Covers

Covers are manufactured from Acrylonitrile Styrene Acrylate (ASA) — the same material used in automotive exterior trim. Expected lifespan exceeds 70 years outdoors. No cover has broken or fallen off in up to 4 years of field deployment.

Ongoing

Voltage & Risk Research

MBCC is conducting research into the relationship between powerline voltage and electrocution risk, informing prioritization of future mitigation work across Mongolia's distribution network.

Wildlife

Species at Risk

Primary species affected include Saker Falcon (Falco cherrug), Steppe Eagle (Aquila nipalensis), and other large raptors. The steppe zone's high rodent density creates concentrated electrocution risk along distribution lines.

Monitoring

Casualty Monitoring

MBCC records wildlife casualties during all field inspections. Data on electrocuted birds — species, age, sex where determinable — feeds into national mortality databases and conservation assessments.
